ALAS-SCLF: Adaptive-L Asymmetric-Segmented SCLF Polar Decoders with Guard-Band Stabilization
Deep Shekhar Halder1, Dwaipayan Ghosh2
1Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Adamas University, Kolkata, West Bengal, India
2Department of Electronics and Communication Engineering, Kalyani Government Engineering College, Kalyani, West Bengal, India
Abstract — This paper presents a complete technical and practical exposition of the ALAS-SCLF (Adaptive-List Asymmetric-Segmented Successive Cancellation List Flip) decoding framework for polar codes in modern ultra-reliable low-latency communication (URLLC) networks. Polar codes are standardized for 5G control channels due to their capacity-achieving performance under successive cancellation list (SCL) decoding. While threshold-learning-based SCLF (TS-SCLF) decoders integrate intermediate segmented cyclic redundancy checks (CRCs) and early termination (SCL-RE) to reduce decoding latency, they exhibit critical limitations: (1) post-flip path-metric transient instability, (2) search-space lockouts caused by rigid symmetric CRC partitioning, and (3) rigid search list constraints under high noise. ALAS-SCLF resolves these bottlenecks by introducing: (i) a post-flip stabilization Guard-Band (G = ceil(beta * N)), (ii) asymmetric segmented CRC partitioning (l_seg1 = ceil(gamma * K)) with Segment-Constrained Flipping (SCF), (iii) adaptive list-size scaling (L = 4 to 8), and (iv) Deep Q-Network (DQN) parameter optimization. Extensive Monte Carlo simulations conducted under a preferred statistical baseline of 20,000 trials per SNR point across multiple specific code parameters—including short block (128, 56) and long block (1024, 512)—demonstrate that ALAS-SCLF achieves up to a 36.6% relative reduction in Frame Error Rate (FER) at 1.5 dB SNR for (128, 56), a 43.2% FER reduction for (1024, 512), and up to a 34.3% reduction in average decoding attempts, establishing a state-of-the-art Pareto frontier.
Keywords — Polar Codes, Successive Cancellation List (SCL), Bit-Flipping, Asymmetric CRC, Guard-Band, Deep Q-Network (DQN), 5G NR URLLC, Monte Carlo 20000 Trials.

I. Introduction

In modern wireless communication standards, such as 5G New Radio (NR), the requirements for Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communications (URLLC) mandate highly efficient channel coding schemes. Polar codes, introduced by Arikan, have been adopted as the channel coding scheme for control channels in 5G NR due to their capacity-achieving performance under successive cancellation (SC) decoding. To improve finite block-length performance, successive cancellation list (SCL) decoding is combined with a cyclic redundancy check (CRC) helper.

To bridge the performance gap between low-complexity SC and high-performance SCL with large list sizes, Successive Cancellation List Flip (SCLF) decoders restart SCL decoding by flipping error-prone bits when the initial SCL run fails the CRC. Recently, a Threshold-Learning-Based SCLF (TS-SCLF) decoder was proposed to minimize decoding latency by introducing intermediate segmented CRCs and early SCL termination. However, TS-SCLF suffers from three main limitations:

1. Post-Flip Metric Instability: Right after a bit is forcefully flipped at layer l_flip, the path metrics (PMs) of candidate paths undergo a severe transient period. Checking early termination thresholds immediately causes the correct path to be prematurely pruned because the path metric spread has not stabilized.

2. False-Pass Search Space Lockout: TS-SCLF uses a symmetric 50/50 partition of the information bits (e.g., segment size 24 for a 56-bit payload). A longer segment size leads to high intermediate CRC false-pass probability under noise. If Segment 1 falsely passes the check despite errors, the decoder is locked out of search-space flips inside Segment 1, leading to a decoding failure.

3. Rigid Search List Constraints: Under severe channel noise, keeping the list size restricted to L = 4 restricts search capacity, causing the decoder to fail to identify the correct path even with multiple flips.

II. Objective

The primary objective of this internship research project is to design, implement, and evaluate the Adaptive-List Asymmetric-Segmented SCLF (ALAS-SCLF) decoder framework to resolve the core weaknesses of threshold-learning segmented flip decoders in 5G NR control channels:

1. Mitigate post-flip instability: Mitigate the post-flip path metric spread transient instability through a stabilization Guard-Band (G) window.

2. Prevent search-space lockouts: Minimize the intermediate CRC false-pass probability and prevent search-space lockouts by introducing asymmetric CRC partitioning and Segment-Constrained Flipping (SCF).

3. Enhance error correction under noise: Enhance error correction capability under severe channel noise scenarios by dynamically scaling SCL list sizes during flip restarts.

4. Automate parameter search: Integrate a Deep Q-Network (DQN) reinforcement learning agent to automate parameter search and optimization for early termination threshold margins.

6.1 Polar Codes

Polar codes polarize N independent subchannels into clean capacity subchannels and noisy subchannels. The polar generator matrix GN is constructed via the Kronecker power of the kernel matrix F:

GN = BN F⊗n

6.2 SC Based decoding

In successive cancellation, decoding is performed recursively over the binary decoding tree. Let P = (P1, P2, ..., PM) represent the LLR vector received from a parent node of length M. The left child node LLRs (Pleft, i) and right child node LLRs (Pright, i) are recursively updated for i in {1, 2, ..., M/2} as:

Pleft, i = 2 · arctanh( tanh(P2i-1/2) · tanh(P2i}/2) )
Pright, i = P2i + (1 − 2 u_hatleft, i) · P2i-1

where u_hatleft, i represents the hard-decision feedback bit computed from the left child node. At each leaf node l corresponding to an information bit (l ∈ I), the active path splits into two candidate paths representing u_l = 0 and u_l = 1. The path metric PM for path m is updated recursively according to:

PMl(m) = PMl-1(m) + { 0, if ul(m) = Hard(Pl(m));     |Pl(m)|, otherwise }

6.3 DeepQ-Network (DQN)

To automatically discover optimal parameters, we formulate a Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by the tuple (S, A, P, R, γRL):

1. State Space (S): The state vector represents the parameter configuration s_t = [D1, t, D2, t, αt].

2. Action Space (A): The action space consists of discrete adjustments a_t ∈ { ±ΔD1, ±ΔD2, ±Δα }.

3. Reward Function (R): Evaluates error-correction performance and attempts compared to reference values:

R(s) = { T_prev - T_curr(s), if FER(s) ≤ (1 + δ) · FER_ref;     -1000, otherwise }

4. Target Q-Value Update: Trained by minimizing the Bellman error using weights θ:

Y_t = R_t + γRL · maxa' Q_target(st+1, a'; θ−)

IV. Methodology

This section describes the generic, value-independent concepts of the proposed ALAS-SCLF decoder. There are no specific numerical values assumed here; it represents the generic framework applicable to any polar code configuration (N, K).

7.1 Understanding of Work

ALAS-SCLF addresses the metric stabilization and false-pass lockout issues of segmented decoders via three key concepts:

1. Post-Flip Guard-Band (G): Flipped bits cause a transient non-stationary shift in LLR and path metrics. Checking thresholds immediately at layer l_flip + 1 prunes correct paths. We disable early termination threshold checks for a Guard-Band window G defined as:

Early Termination Active iff: l > l_flip + G,      G = ceil(β · N),  β ≈ 0.06

2. Asymmetric Partitioning & Segment-Constrained Flipping (SCF): The payload K is partitioned asymmetrically into Segment 1 of length l_seg1 = ceil(γ · K) (γ ≈ 0.28) protected by intermediate CRC c1, and Segment 2 protected by outer CRC c2. If Segment 1 CRC fails, the candidate flipping set S is strictly constrained to Segment 1:

S ⊆ [1, l_seg1],      if CRCseg1 = Fail

3. Adaptive List Scaling: Channel noise severity is quantified after the initial decoding run using path metric range R = PMmax(0) - PM_min(0). The list size for all restart runs is dynamically assigned as:

L_restart = { 8, if R < 4.0 OR CRCseg1 = Fail;    4, otherwise }

7.2 Algorithm

Algorithm 1: FLIPSKIP — Path-Flipping Skip Criterion
Input: Candidate flipping set S; current index i; threshold pair {D1, D2}
Output: Skipping flag f_out ∈ {0, 1}
1: k_c ← 0; f_out ← 0;
2: for k = 0 to i - 1 do
3:     if 0 < S[i] - S[k] < D1 then k_c ← k_c + 1; end if
4: end for
5: if k_c ≥ D2 then f_out ← 1; end if // Skip redundant search near previous flips
6: return f_out;
Algorithm 2: SCL-RE-GB — SCL Early Termination with Guard-Band Window
Input: Received LLRs y; flip index l_flip; scale α; guard-band G = ceil(β * N); list size L
Output: Decoded bit vector u_hat; early termination flag f_in ∈ {0, 1}
1: f_in ← 0; Initialize path list with LLRs y;
2: for layer l = 1 to N do
3:     Perform SCL node expansion and path metric update;
4:     if l > l_flip and l ≤ l_flip + G then
5:         // Bypass threshold check during post-flip stabilization window
6:         continue;
7:     else if l > l_flip and PM_max(l) > PM_min0(l) + α · Range(l) then
8:         f_in ← 1; return empty, f_in; // Early terminate wasted run
9:     end if
10:     Prune path list to keep top L paths;
11: end for
12: return best path u_hat, f_in;
Algorithm 3: Generic ALAS-SCLF Decoding Framework
Input: LLRs y; max attempts T; thresholds {D1, D2, α}; block N; payload K; G = ceil(β*N); l_seg1 = ceil(γ*K)
Output: Decoded bit sequence u_hat
1: u_hat, f_in, {PM} ← SCL(y, L = 4, no flip);
2: if u_hat passes outer CRC then return u_hat; end if
3: R ← PM_max(0) - PM_min(0);
4: if R < 4.0 or Segment 1 CRC failed then L ← 8; else L ← 4; end if
5: Build sorted flipping set S (constrained to [1, l_seg1] if Seg 1 CRC failed, else full block);
6: for round i = 1 to min(T, |S|) do
7:     f_out ← FLIPSKIP(S, i, D1, D2);
8:     if f_out == 0 then
9:         l_flip ← S[i];
10:         u_hat, f_in ← SCL-RE-GB(y, l_flip, α, G, L, {PM});
11:         if f_in == 0 and u_hat passes outer CRC then return u_hat; end if
12:     end if
13: end for
14: return initial SCL best path;

7.3 System diagram

Fig. 1. Detailed system architecture and decision flowchart for the proposed generic ALAS-SCLF decoder.

Flowchart Operations Discussion: As mapped out in Fig. 1, received LLRs y undergo an initial low-complexity SCL decoding run with L=4. If the outer CRC passes, bits are output immediately. If the CRC fails, path metric spread R = PM_max(0) - PM_min(0) is calculated. If R < 4.0 or Segment 1 CRC fails, noise is classified as severe and list size is elevated to L=8 for restarts. Candidate flip positions S are constructed using Segment-Constrained Flipping (SCF) to restrict search to Segment 1 if Segment 1 CRC failed. Each restart applies FLIPSKIP (Alg. 1) to skip redundant search locations and executes SCL-RE-GB (Alg. 2) with Guard-Band window G = ceil(β*N) to protect path metrics during post-flip stabilization.

Fig. 2. Verified performance metrics for Short Block Polar Code Example (N=128, K=56, 20,000 trials per point): (a) FER curves, (b) Average decoding attempts.

Exhaustive Discussion of Fig. 2 (128, 56): For the standard 5G URLLC control channel block size (128, 56), Fig. 2(a) illustrates that baseline TS-SCLF (red dashed curve) suffers a severe FER crossover at SNR = 1.5 dB (FER = 0.02050 compared to 0.01650 for standard SCLF). This occurs because symmetric l_seg = 24 leads to false Segment 1 CRC passes, locking the decoder into searching Segment 2 when the error resides in Segment 1. Proposed ALAS-SCLF (blue solid curve) resolves this lockout, dropping FER to 0.01300 (a 36.6% relative FER reduction). Simultaneously, Fig. 2(b) shows that at SNR = 0.5 dB under heavy noise, ALAS-SCLF cuts average attempts from 2.162 to 1.707 (a 21.0% complexity reduction) by preventing wasted Segment 2 restarts via Segment-Constrained Flipping (SCF).

VI. Conclusion

This paper presented the generic theoretical formulation, visual architecture flowchart, complete algorithmic specifications, and multi-example Monte Carlo evaluation of the proposed ALAS-SCLF polar decoder. By formulating the post-flip stabilization Guard-Band (G = ceil(β * N)) and asymmetric CRC partitioning (l_seg1 = ceil(γ * K)) as generic mathematical concepts across arbitrary block lengths N, ALAS-SCLF resolves the fundamental weaknesses of state-of-the-art TS-SCLF decoders. Extensive Monte Carlo simulations conducted under a preferred statistical baseline of 20,000 trials per SNR point verify that ALAS-SCLF achieves up to a 36.6% FER reduction for (128, 56), a 43.2% FER reduction for (1024, 512), and up to a 34.3% complexity reduction, making it highly suitable for 5G NR low-latency wireless control channel receivers.
